About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Data Science in the School of Computing Sciences and Mathematics at Mount Royal University in Calgary, Canada. I hold a PhD in Biomedical Engineering from the University of Calgary. My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, biomedical engineering, and neuroimaging, with a focus on developing machine learning and deep learning methods to better understand brain function and physiology.

A central focus of my research is the development of sensor-free approaches for reconstructing physiological signals directly from functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). My work investigates respiratory and cardiovascular dynamics, cerebrovascular reactivity, and physiological influences on brain imaging, aiming to improve the accuracy, accessibility, and interpretation of neuroimaging data.

My broader research interests include AI for brain health and aging, biomedical image analysis, physiological signal reconstruction, and reproducible computational methods. I am particularly interested in developing generalizable AI models that can be applied across different populations and neuroimaging datasets.

Alongside my research, I am passionate about teaching and mentoring students in machine learning, data science, scientific programming, and biomedical AI, helping them develop the skills to conduct independent, interdisciplinary research.
